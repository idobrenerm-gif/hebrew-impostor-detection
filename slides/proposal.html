~20 sec. Suggested speaker: Ido (split not agreed yet). Introduce the team and the one-line idea: we detect LLM-written posts that impersonate Israelis on Hebrew social media. The post on the slide is an illustrative mock-up, not real data.

Motivating use case

Foreign influence operations run profiles that pose as Israelis.

More and more of what they post is written by LLMs.

Why it matters

Fake “Israeli voices” can steer public discussion on Hebrew social media.

Why it’s hard

LLM text is fluent and on-topic. There is no labeled Hebrew data for this task, and a classifier can learn topic or model style instead of impersonation.

How it’s handled today

Platform moderation and manual investigation. Generic AI-text detectors answer “AI or human?”, not “is this really an Israeli?”.

Pipeline end to end: generate impostor posts with several LLMs and Israeli personas and collect authentic posts; match topics between the classes; train or prompt four methods on the same data; evaluate at post and account level and across generators. Methods: three trained on our data (TF-IDF + LR baseline, fine-tuned DictaBERT/AlephBERT, LoRA on a small LLM) vs. one off-the-shelf (LLM zero-shot / few-shot). Hebrew Impostor Detection · Proposal

4 / 5

~1 min. Suggested speaker: Oren. Impostor class: synthetic posts from several LLMs with Israeli personas; which generators, and which are held out, is open. Optional extension: machine-translated Hebrew from Arabic, Persian or English; not yet decided for v1. Authentic class: real Israeli posts from an existing dataset if we find one, otherwise collected manually by us; source and topic recorded for every post. Labels come from how each post was produced. Topic matching across classes is our main defense against shortcut learning.
